## Challenge

High component count and variable build conditions increased risk of defects, missed steps, and inconsistent assembly outcomes.

## Constraints

- Large BOM and frequent engineering updates.
- High traceability expectations.
- Need for practical instructions usable by production teams.

## My Responsibility

I owned the translation of engineering and quality requirements into practical build strategy and production-facing instructions, then maintained that system as designs and shop-floor learning evolved.

## Approach and Decisions

- Developed production strategies and build sequences for 1,000+ component assemblies.
- Authored and maintained detailed work instructions with revision discipline.
- Integrated mistake-proofing and quality checkpoints to reduce nonconformance risk.

Instructions were organized around the decisions an operator needed at each stage: prerequisites, sequence, acceptance criteria, traceability, and escalation points. This made the plan useful at the workstation while preserving the rigor needed for configuration control.

## Verification

- Reviewed instructions with engineering, quality, and production stakeholders.
- Used build feedback and nonconformance learning to revise sequence and controls.
- Positioned inspection and hold points at risk-significant steps rather than relying only on end-of-process detection.

## Outcomes

- Improved process clarity and repeatability across teams.
- Enabled faster onboarding and smoother execution of complex builds.
- Reduced avoidable defects through proactive process controls.

## What I Learned

Detailed instructions are most effective when they are treated as a living production system: controlled enough to be trustworthy, but easy to improve when new evidence emerges.
